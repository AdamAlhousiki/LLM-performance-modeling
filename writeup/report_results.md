## Closed-form vs Markov-chain-lite

We also implemented a Markov-chain-lite model over the total number of admitted
requests in the system, `n = 0, ..., N`, where `N = floor(K / E[kv])`. This is a
finite birth-death chain with Poisson births below the KV cap and
state-dependent death rates based on `active_batch = min(n, B)`. The stationary
distribution is solved from birth-death ratios with safe normalization.

| Metric | Closed-form mean relative error | Markov-lite mean relative error | Closed-form median relative error | Markov-lite median relative error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| mean TTFT | 0.2315 | 0.2315 | 0.0946 | 0.0946 |
| p95 TTFT | 0.4568 | 2.0955 | 0.4489 | 0.4484 |
| p99 TTFT | 0.6317 | 2.0518 | 0.5950 | 0.5214 |
| goodput | 0.0892 | 0.0892 | 0.0870 | 0.0870 |
| blocking probability | 0.4896 | 0.4896 | 0.0037 | 0.0037 |

Interpretation: Markov-lite uses the same one-dimensional occupancy chain as
the closed-form model for blocking, goodput, and mean occupancy, so those
metrics match closely. The difference is in tail modeling. The closed-form
model uses a more explicit Erlang wait-time mixture, while Markov-lite uses a
simpler exponential-tail approximation. As a result, Markov-lite is competitive
on median p99 error but worse on mean p95/p99 error because a few overload
points produce large tail overestimates.

### Calibrated tail correction

As a third analytical baseline, we added a calibrated Markov-tail correction.
This is not a pure closed-form model. It keeps Markov-lite mean TTFT, goodput,
and blocking unchanged, then fits a lightweight linear model on the log residual
`log(simulated tail / Markov-lite tail)` for p95 and p99 TTFT. The features are
configuration-level quantities such as arrival rate, batch size, KV budget,
Markov blocking probability, Markov goodput, and simple load interactions. Rows
are sorted by configuration and every fourth row is held out as test data.

Overall relative errors:

| metric | Closed-form mean | Markov-lite mean | Calibrated-tail mean | Closed-form median | Markov-lite median | Calibrated-tail median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| p95 TTFT | 0.4568 | 2.0955 | 0.5349 | 0.4489 | 0.4484 | 0.2331 |
| p99 TTFT | 0.6317 | 2.0518 | 0.6744 | 0.5950 | 0.5214 | 0.3418 |

Train/test relative errors for the calibrated tail model:

| metric | split | Closed-form mean | Markov-lite mean | Calibrated-tail mean | Closed-form median | Markov-lite median | Calibrated-tail median |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| p95 TTFT | train | 0.4522 | 1.5045 | 0.3061 | 0.4500 | 0.2904 | 0.1974 |
| p95 TTFT | test | 0.4704 | 3.8684 | 1.2212 | 0.4354 | 0.9750 | 0.3794 |
| p99 TTFT | train | 0.6340 | 1.6625 | 0.4235 | 0.5962 | 0.5214 | 0.2783 |
| p99 TTFT | test | 0.6247 | 3.2197 | 1.4273 | 0.5950 | 0.5523 | 0.6171 |

Interpretation: the calibrated model improves the median p95/p99 tail errors
and substantially reduces the large Markov-lite train errors. The held-out mean
error is still high for a small number of difficult tail configurations, so the
claim should be modest: average behavior is analytically tractable, while tail
latency benefits from calibration and ultimately needs a richer state-aware
model or more empirical data.

### Stable and overloaded regimes

To test whether the large held-out mean errors are dominated by overloaded
configurations, we split rows using simulator blocking probability:
`stable = blocking_probability_sim < 0.05` and
`overloaded = blocking_probability_sim >= 0.05`. The table below comes from
`results/calibrated_tail_error_by_regime.csv`.

| metric | method | split | stable mean | overloaded mean | stable median | overloaded median |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| p95 TTFT | closed-form | train | 0.5313 | 0.1753 | 0.4642 | 0.1570 |
| p95 TTFT | closed-form | test | 0.3861 | 0.4872 | 0.3861 | 0.4469 |
| p95 TTFT | Markov-lite | train | 1.0355 | 3.1459 | 0.1959 | 2.9935 |
| p95 TTFT | Markov-lite | test | 0.1509 | 4.6119 | 0.1509 | 1.6119 |
| p95 TTFT | calibrated-tail | train | 0.2930 | 0.3519 | 0.1792 | 0.3962 |
| p95 TTFT | calibrated-tail | test | 0.1776 | 1.4300 | 0.1776 | 0.4588 |
| p99 TTFT | closed-form | train | 0.7371 | 0.2734 | 0.6830 | 0.2600 |
| p99 TTFT | closed-form | test | 0.5237 | 0.6450 | 0.5237 | 0.6078 |
| p99 TTFT | Markov-lite | train | 1.3248 | 2.8446 | 0.3423 | 2.6025 |
| p99 TTFT | Markov-lite | test | 0.1168 | 3.8403 | 0.1168 | 1.1730 |
| p99 TTFT | calibrated-tail | train | 0.4595 | 0.2974 | 0.2852 | 0.2595 |
| p99 TTFT | calibrated-tail | test | 0.4792 | 1.6169 | 0.4792 | 0.6171 |

This confirms that the largest held-out Markov-lite and calibrated-tail mean
errors are concentrated in overloaded configurations. In stable test rows, the
calibrated-tail mean relative error is 0.1776 for p95 TTFT and 0.4792 for p99
TTFT. In overloaded test rows, those means rise to 1.4300 and 1.6169,
respectively. The overloaded medians are much smaller than the overloaded means,
so a few difficult saturated points dominate the average.

- Model: `Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct`
- vLLM version: `0.22.0`
- GPU class: Modal A10G
- Arrival rates: 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 req/s
- Max sequence counts: 64, 128
- GPU memory utilization settings: 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9
- Realized KV cache range: 104,336 to 614,736 tokens

- Measured requests per row: 300

### vLLM Observations

- Goodput rises approximately linearly with offered load over the measured
  range, reaching 6.74 req/s at 8 req/s offered load.
- TTFT remains low on this small model: mean TTFT stays below 61 ms and p99
  TTFT stays below 185 ms across all measured rows.
- TPOT increases with load from about 8.3 ms/token to about 10.2 ms/token,
  showing the expected decode-step contention as active concurrency increases.
- Realized KV changed substantially with memory-utilization settings, but this
  measured grid still did not trigger request failures or vLLM preemptions:
  request failures and preemption count were zero in all rows.

Report takeaway: the current hardware sweep validates the end-to-end
Modal-to-vLLM measurement loop and gives real TPOT/TTFT scale for Qwen2.5-1.5B.
The stress sweep additionally demonstrates queueing pressure, but neither sweep
triggered KV-cache preemption or request failures.

### vLLM Stress Sweep

The original 64-row vLLM sweep stayed below saturation: request failures and
preemption count were zero in every measured row. We therefore added a separate
stress harness,
`experiments/run_vllm_stress_sweep.py`, rather than replacing the original
measurement set.

The stress configuration is intentionally tiny: arrival rates 12 and 24 req/s,
`max_num_seqs=64`, GPU memory utilization 0.30, `max_model_len=4096`, and 200
measured requests plus 4 warmup requests per row. Its goal is to test whether
real vLLM begins queueing, preempting, or failing requests under heavier
pressure without spending hours on a large sweep. It writes
`results/vllm_stress_summary.csv` with TTFT, TPOT, goodput, preemption count,
request failures, realized KV cache, and active-concurrency fields. The
associated plots are
`figures/vllm_stress_ttft.png`, `figures/vllm_stress_tpot.png`, and
`figures/vllm_stress_goodput.png`.

Tiny stress results:

| arrival rate (req/s) | mean TTFT (s) | p95 TTFT (s) | p99 TTFT (s) | mean TPOT (ms/token) | goodput (req/s) | peak concurrency | failures | preemptions |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 0.0558 | 0.1005 | 0.3198 | 11.38 | 9.10 | 68 | 0 | 0 |
| 24 | 0.9424 | 2.4022 | 2.4851 | 12.22 | 11.70 | 121 | 0 | 0 |

Interpretation: the stress sweep still did not trigger request failures or
preemptions, but it did expose queueing pressure. Moving from 12 to 24 req/s
increased mean TTFT from 56 ms to 942 ms and p99 TTFT from 320 ms to 2.49 s.
TPOT increased only modestly, so most of the degradation appears in waiting
time before first token rather than per-token decode time.

## SimPy vs vLLM Interpretation

The SimPy and vLLM sweeps should not be plotted as a strict one-to-one
calibration yet:

- SimPy uses synthetic lognormal lengths and simplified iteration latency.
- vLLM uses real ShareGPT prompts and real A10G serving behavior.
- The SimPy sweep currently covers smaller B/K regimes than the vLLM sweep.
- vLLM does not expose blocking in the same way as the SimPy admission model;
  it queues and may preempt instead of immediately rejecting at arrival.

The fair comparison for the report is qualitative:

1. Both systems show goodput increasing with offered load.
2. vLLM's measured latency scale is much lower than the original toy SimPy
   constants, motivating the measured `results/latency_curve.json` calibration.
3. The analytical model should be calibrated to measured service rates before
   claiming quantitative agreement with hardware.
4. KV preemption/failure remains a follow-up measurement target; the hardware
   grid did not reach a KV-cache preemption regime, but the stress sweep did
   demonstrate queueing pressure.

## Suggested Final Report Claims

- We built a reusable SimPy simulator for continuous batching with finite KV
  budget, Poisson arrivals, prompt/generation lengths, TTFT, goodput, and
  blocking metrics.
- We implemented a closed-form analytical approximation and comparison harness.
  It predicts goodput reasonably well but needs a richer tail model for p95/p99
  TTFT under overload.
- We created a canonical ShareGPT trace so simulation and vLLM measurements can
  replay the same request-length pool.
- We measured vLLM on Modal/A10G with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. The measured sweep
  completed 64 benchmark rows and showed mean TTFT below 61 ms, p99 TTFT below
  185 ms, and TPOT around 8 to 10 ms/token.
- The current hardware grid did not produce request failures or preemption; the
  stress sweep did demonstrate queueing pressure, and future work should use
  longer traces or larger models to force KV contention.

## Suggested Limitations Paragraph

The simulator and analytical model are intentionally simplified. They model a
single GPU and a single model, assume Poisson arrivals, and approximate vLLM's
continuous batching at an iteration level. The first analytical baseline uses
average request sizes and an approximate tail distribution, so it is less
accurate for p95/p99 TTFT in overload regimes. The vLLM measurements validate
the empirical pipeline and provide real latency scale. The stress sweep shows
queueing pressure, but neither sweep reached a KV-cache preemption/failure
regime; therefore, hardware KV-contention validation remains future work.
